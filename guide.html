The Complete MSNR + Price Action Masterclass

This is the exact methodology the BullBytes signal engine runs on, in plain language. No indicators. Just structure, levels, liquidity and discipline.

Part 1 · MSNR — Malaysian Support & Resistance

What MSNR actually is

Normal support/resistance with one massive upgrade: you only care about candle bodies, never wicks.

A candle's body shows where buyers and sellers actually agreed and settled. The wick is a quick spike — emotion, panic, stop hunting. Body levels mark where the real money sat down; wick levels mark where someone panicked for five seconds.

Rule Zero of MSNR: open and close prices matter. Highs and lows don't.

How to draw an MSNR level

  1. Open the H4 chart on gold.
  2. Find a spot where price made a strong move away — a big push up or down.
  3. Look at the candle where that move started.
  4. Draw the line at the body of that candle (open or close), not the wick tip.
  5. Extend it to the right. That's your level. No indicators, no EMA, no RSI — just lines.

A level isn't a hairline — treat it as a small zone, roughly $2–3 thick on gold. Price respects areas, not exact prices.

Fresh vs Used vs Dead — the most important concept

  • Fresh level — price created it, ran away, and has never come back. All the pending institutional orders are untouched. Full fuel tank. First touch = strongest reaction = A+ trade.
  • Used level — touched once, bounced, left. Some fuel remains. Second touch = okay trade.
  • Dead level — tested 3+ times. When price keeps knocking on the same door, the door eventually breaks. Don't trade it — expect a breakout instead.

The Flip — your bread-and-butter setup

The setup you'll trade most of the time:

  1. Price has a support level (drawn from bodies).
  2. A candle closes its body below that support — not a wick poke, a full body close. That's a real break.
  3. The level changes its job: old support is now resistance. Everyone who bought there is trapped, and when price returns they sell to escape at breakeven — that selling pressure is the new resistance.
  4. Wait for price to pull back up and retest the broken level.
  5. Rejection at the retest → enter SELL. Mirror logic for broken resistance → buy the retest.
Most people lose not because the flip fails, but because they can't wait for the retest and jump in early. The retest is the trade.

Gap SNR — the king level

When price moves so fast it leaves a gap between candle bodies, nobody got to trade in that space — zero negotiation. Markets hate unfinished business: price very often returns to fill the zone, then reacts violently. A Gap SNR that is also fresh is the highest-probability level in the entire system. Mark it and wait like a sniper.

A-shape and V-shape levels

  • V-shape: price crashes, instantly reverses and rockets up. That bottom (the body of the reversal candle) is a powerful buy level — someone with deep pockets bought aggressively there.
  • A-shape: price spikes up and instantly dumps. That top is a strong sell level.

Sharp reversals = institutional footprints. Slow, rounded reversals = retail noise. Mark the sharp ones.

Part 2 · Advanced Price Action

Market structure — the skeleton of everything

Price only ever does three things: up, down, or sideways.

  • Uptrend: higher highs + higher lows. Stairs going up.
  • Downtrend: lower highs + lower lows. Stairs going down.
  • Range: peaks and dips at roughly the same levels.
In an uptrend you ONLY look for buys at support. In a downtrend, ONLY sells at resistance. Counter-trend trading is how accounts die. The trend is not your friend — it's your boss.

BOS and CHoCH — when structure speaks

  • BOS (Break of Structure): price breaks the previous peak in trend direction → "yes, we're still going". Stay with the trend.
  • CHoCH (Change of Character): first break against the trend → the market's first crack. Stop buying, start watching for sells (or vice versa).

Liquidity — the stop-hunt game

Big players need your stop losses to fill their orders. A bank that wants to buy massive size can't just click buy — there aren't enough sellers. So they push price down into the zones where retail stops cluster (a triggered buy-side stop is a sell order), absorb them as their fill, and then price launches.

Where do stops cluster? Below obvious supports, above obvious resistances, and especially at equal highs / equal lows — clean double tops/bottoms look "safe", so everyone hides stops there.

Your edge: never enter at the level directly. Wait for the sweep first. A sweep into a fresh MSNR level means the trap has been sprung — now you enter with the big players instead of being their fuel.

Confirmation candles — your green light (M15)

  • Engulfing: a candle that completely swallows the previous body in the opposite direction.
  • Pin bar: long wick through the level, small body closing back inside — the market tried to break it and got slapped back.
  • Close back inside: price dipped through the level (sweep) but the candle closed back beyond it. Fakeout confirmed.
Golden discipline rule: wait for the candle to fully CLOSE. A candle can look like a perfect rejection at minute 12 and become a full breakdown by minute 15. Closed candles are facts; open candles are rumours.

Sessions — when to trade gold (PKT)

  • Asia (~5am–12pm PKT): gold sleeps. Don't trade it — but mark the Asia high/low; they become liquidity targets.
  • London (12pm–4pm PKT): gold wakes up. London's first move is very often the FAKE one — it sweeps the Asia range.
  • New York (5:30pm–10pm PKT): the real session. Classic play: London sweeps one side of the Asia range, New York drives the true move the other way.

If you only traded 5:30–9pm PKT and ignored everything else, you'd already be ahead of most traders.

Part 3 · Stop Loss — the complete guide

What an SL really is

A stop loss is not an insult to your analysis. It's the price where your trade idea is officially wrong. If you bought because support should hold and price closes well below it, your reason for being in the trade is gone — the SL just automates the exit so emotions can't keep you holding a corpse.

Trading gold without an SL is not brave — it's slow account deletion. Gold moves $20–40 on a normal day; one news spike against you can erase weeks of profit in minutes.

Where exactly to place it

  • Buy: below the low of the rejection/sweep candle, plus a 30–50 pip buffer ($3–5). Gold loves poking exact lows by a dollar before reversing.
  • Sell: above the rejection candle's high + the same buffer.
  • Never at a round number ($4200.00, $4250.00) or exactly at an obvious high/low — that's where everyone else's stop sits, which is exactly where price will visit.

Lot size — the math that saves your account

Losing traders pick the lot size first, then squeeze the SL to fit. That's backwards. Correct order:

  1. Decide your risk: 1–2% of the account. $500 at 1% = $5. $1000 at 2% = $20.
  2. Measure the SL from the chart: the structure tells you where it belongs — say 50 pips.
  3. Calculate the lot: Risk ÷ (SL pips × pip value). On gold 1 pip = $1 per 0.10 lot, so $10 ÷ 50 pips = 0.02 lots.
The chart decides the SL placement. Your account size decides the lot. You never compromise the SL to fit a bigger lot — you shrink the lot to fit the proper SL. (Use the position size calculator.)

The unbreakable rules

  1. Never widen the SL. Moving it away "to give the trade room" isn't trading — it's praying.
  2. Know SL and TP before entry. If you can't state them before clicking, you have a gamble, not a trade.
  3. Minimum 1:2 reward-to-risk, with the TP at a real level. At 1:2 you only need a 34% win rate to break even.
  4. Breakeven move: once price moves 1R in your favour, slide the SL to entry. Worst case becomes zero.
  5. One loss = stop, breathe. Two losses in a day = close the laptop. Revenge trading turns a $20 loss into a $200 one.

The full system in one flow

  1. H4: what's the structure — up, down, or range?
  2. H4/H1: mark FRESH body-based levels in the trend's direction. Gaps and flips = priority.
  3. Wait for price to actually reach a level. This can take hours or days — that's normal.
  4. Watch for a liquidity sweep into the level.
  5. M15: wait for a CLOSED confirmation candle — engulfing, pin bar, or close back inside.
  6. Enter. SL behind the sweep wick + $3–5 buffer. Lot sized from your 1–2% risk.
  7. TP ladder: 1 SL, 3 TPs — partials at TP1, breakeven, let the runners go. Minimum 1:2 overall.
  8. No setup = no trade. Sitting on your hands IS a position.
The hard part isn't the knowledge — you now have all of it. The hard part is waiting for steps 3, 4 and 5 without jumping early. The market pays patient snipers and eats impatient machine-gunners.
